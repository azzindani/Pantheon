6-HOUR BRIEFING — the deal at the edge.

First: run `date '+%A %d %b %Y %H:%M %Z'` to anchor the exact time of this run. Prioritize sources and data published in the last 24-48 hours — always note the publication date or how recent each source is. Do not reuse topics already covered in your recent memory.

Deliver ONE deep, current dispatch on dealmaking and negotiation: a tactic, a leverage read, or a piece of deal choreography that wins. Think in interests and leverage, not just price — what does each side actually want, and what can they walk to? Tie it to a CURRENT real deal, dispute, or standoff in the news.

Each run, pick a DIFFERENT angle you have not covered recently (consult your memory; rotate widely). Rotate across, and go beyond, this menu — be proactive and surface things I did not ask for:
- Negotiation strategy: mapping interests vs. positions, BATNA, the sequence of moves and concessions
- M&A and commercial choreography: term sheets, counters, earnouts, CVRs, price/risk trade-offs, go-shop and break-fee games
- Leverage reads on a live deal — who has the better walk-away, who's on the clock, who blinks
- Persuasion mechanics: framing, anchoring, reciprocity, the memo/script that actually lands for a specific audience
- Stakeholder alignment: boards, lenders, regulators, activists — anticipating objections before they're raised
- A famous deal teardown: what the winning side did at the table, decoded into a repeatable move

To research, run python3 /opt/data/bin/websearch.py 'your query' (ranked web results with titles, URLs and snippets from the local SearXNG) and python3 /opt/data/bin/webfetch.py 'URL' to read a source; skip obvious spam/gibberish results and prefer credible sources. Ground it in CURRENT real deals, filings, and reporting — do not rely on memory alone.

Deliver, as a tight Telegram briefing (under ~3500 chars, short paragraphs/bullets):
1. The situation: the parties, the stakes, and each side's real interest
2. The leverage map — BATNAs, constraints, and who's on the clock
3. The move: the play, the likely counter, and the prepared re-counter (one step ahead)
4. A current real-world instance — cite the source date
5. SOURCE LINKS (full URLs) at the end
Finish with one sharper opening move of your own design.
