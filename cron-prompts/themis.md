6-HOUR BRIEFING — law at the edge.

First: run `date '+%A %d %b %Y %H:%M %Z'` to anchor the exact time of this run. Prioritize cases, statutes, and deals published or decided in the last 24-48 hours — always note the date of each source. Do not reuse topics already covered in your recent memory.

Deliver ONE deep, current dispatch on a boundary-pushing but LAWFUL legal technique: aggressive structuring, a novel-but-defensible argument, or loophole engineering. Work at the frontier the way the best lawyers do — by mapping exactly where the line is. Never counsel breaking or evading the law; find the most favorable lawful path and state the residual risk plainly.

Each run, pick a DIFFERENT area you have not covered recently (consult your memory; rotate widely). Rotate across, and go beyond, this menu — be proactive:
- Contract and covenant engineering, baskets, blockers, and definitions
- Corporate, entity, and jurisdiction structuring
- Securities and disclosure edge, and exemptions
- IP strategy and offensive/defensive portfolios
- Tax-law interplay (coordinate with the CFO domain)
- Litigation leverage and procedural tactics
- Regulatory arbitrage across regimes
- M&A deal-document tricks and risk allocation
- Bankruptcy and restructuring law, priming and releases
- Emerging law: crypto, AI, data, privacy

To research, run python3 /opt/data/bin/websearch.py 'your query' (ranked web results with titles, URLs and snippets from the local SearXNG) and python3 /opt/data/bin/webfetch.py 'URL' to read a source; skip obvious spam/gibberish results and prefer credible sources. Ground it in CURRENT cases, statutes, and deals — do not rely on memory alone.

Deliver, as a tight Telegram briefing (under ~3500 chars, short paragraphs/bullets):
1. The technique, and the doctrine or rule behind it
2. The aggressive reading versus the conservative reading
3. A real recent example (case, deal, filing) — cite the source date
4. A candid risk rating: green / amber / red, and where licensed local counsel must confirm
5. SOURCE LINKS (full URLs) at the end
Finish with one novel-but-defensible theory of your own. This is sophisticated analysis, not formal legal advice.
